# Krama — A Guided Walk for Teachers
*Thank you for being one of the first three teachers to try this. Your honest reactions — especially the uncomfortable ones — are the most valuable thing you can give.*

---

## Before you begin

**What Krama is:** a small app for planning yoga classes. You build a sequence from a pose library, arrange it into the arc of a class, and read from it when you teach.

**What it is not (yet):** there are no accounts and nothing to sign up for. Your sequences live on your own phone or laptop. Some features are intentionally missing — this is an early version, and what's missing is part of what I'm asking you about.

**What you need:** your phone (best) or a laptop, about 45 minutes total, in one sitting or several. Open the link I sent you in your browser — that's it.

**How to share feedback:** as you go, jot answers to the prompts below — voice memos, texts, or scribbles in a notebook are all perfect. Please don't polish anything. "This annoyed me" is more useful than a paragraph of kindness.

**One request:** narrate your confusion. The moment you think "wait, where do I…?" — that exact sentence is gold. Write it down before you figure it out.

---

## Walk 1 · Meet the poses (~10 min)

1. Open the app and tap **Poses**.
2. Find a pose you teach every week, any way that feels natural.
3. Open it and read what's there.
4. Tap the chips at the top — **simple**, **advanced**, **expert** — and watch what appears and disappears.
5. Now find one pose you *rarely* teach, and read its page too.

**Tell me:**
- Did the pose page use words the way you use them in your teaching? Where did the vocabulary feel off, or feel like it was written by someone who doesn't practice?
- Was anything missing that you'd want to know before teaching that pose to a real room?
- Which of the three views (simple / advanced / expert) would you actually live in — and did anything in the deeper views feel like noise?

---

## Walk 2 · Build a short class from scratch (~15 min)

1. Tap **Compose** and start a new flow. Name it anything.
2. Build a 30-minute class you might genuinely teach — add poses, set breaths or time for each, drag things around until the order feels right.
3. Notice the small lines *between* poses — the app's sense of how smoothly one pose moves into the next. Ignore them or argue with them, as you like.
4. Add a moment of stillness somewhere (there's a button for it).
5. If a small amber note appears at the bottom, read it. Do what it says, or don't.

**Tell me:**
- Where did building feel like planning a class, and where did it feel like operating software?
- The smoothness lines between poses: did they match your body's sense of those transitions? Name one place the app was wrong.
- Did the app ever try to be the teacher instead of the assistant? Where?
- What did you reach for that wasn't there? (First three things.)

---

## Walk 3 · Borrow and make it yours (~10 min)

1. Go to **Flows** and open the built-in **60-minute vinyasa**.
2. Duplicate it, then change it until it's a class *you* would teach — swap poses, retime things, cut what you'd never do.
3. Now open the **60-minute yin** flow and just read through it without editing.

**Tell me:**
- Was the built-in class credible — could a real teacher have written it? What gave it away, either direction?
- What was harder than it should have been while reshaping it?
- Yin teachers especially: does the way this app holds long stillness and rebounds respect the practice, or does it still think like a vinyasa app?

---

## Walk 4 · Teach from it (~10 min, on the mat if you can)

1. Open any flow you made and switch to the **read view**.
2. Put the phone where you'd put it while teaching — on the mat, on a stool — and move through at least ten minutes of the sequence, cueing out loud or silently.
3. Notice every time you have to touch, squint at, or fight the screen.

**Tell me:**
- Could you glance at it mid-pose and get what you needed in one look? When couldn't you?
- Did the breath markings help you cue, or did you ignore them?
- Would you actually bring this into a room of students next week? What would have to change first — one thing.

---

## Closing questions (after all four walks)

1. In one sentence: what is this app *for*? (I want to hear it in your words, not mine.)
2. What's the one thing you'd remove?
3. What's the one thing you'd add before you'd show it to a teacher friend?
4. Was there a moment it felt beautiful — made *for* yoga rather than merely about it? Was there a moment it broke that spell?
5. Would you use it for your own planning next month, honestly? Why or why not?
